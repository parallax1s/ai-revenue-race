The AI revenue race

Anthropic passed OpenAI in annualized revenue around April 2026 — $30B against a roughly flat ~$25B — and kept climbing to $47B by mid-May, an estimated ~$63B exiting June, and a confirmed $65B at the end of July. That confirmation also means Anthropic's own growth cooled sharply in July, to low single digits month-over-month. OpenAI meanwhile reaccelerated hard: Bloomberg puts its run rate above $40B as of August 13. Microsoft's disclosed "AI business" — Azure AI plus Copilot — crossed $37B in April. Everyone else — Grok, Mistral, Perplexity, Cohere, DeepSeek — sums to roughly $2B combined. Google and Meta never break the number out, so they ride the chart as dashed-gray estimates: ~$3–6B of direct Gemini revenue and ~$0.1–0.3B for Meta AI — while their real AI money hides in ads Google says AI Mode monetizes "at similar rates to Search," and Meta's AI ad suite runs at $75B a year.

The race, in three acts

Through 2025 OpenAI led comfortably — $10B annualized in June 2025 against Anthropic's $3B that May, and $20B by year-end against Anthropic's $9B. In early 2026 the lines converged violently: Anthropic went $14B (Feb 12) → $30B (Apr 6) → $47B (mid-May) while OpenAI sat roughly flat near $25B from February to April — the crossing lands in April. Act three is a double deceleration: Anthropic confirmed to investors a $65B run rate at the end of July — up only ~3% from the ~$63B estimated June exit, after months above 20% — while Bloomberg reported OpenAI's July growth above 20% month-over-month and a rate topping $40B on August 13, driven by coding tools, subscriptions, and its new ads business. The gap held (~1.6×), but it is now OpenAI that is compounding faster.

Was June an Anthropic slowdown?

In growth-rate terms, mildly — the arithmetic forces it. Interpolating the run-rate checkpoints gives April ≈ $2.8B and May ≈ $4.0B of monthly revenue; Bloomberg's preliminary "more than $11.5B" Q2 total leaves June ≈ $4.7–4.9B, i.e. +20–30% month-over-month versus ~+40% in April–May. Had the pace held, June would have been ~$5.6B and Q2 ~$12.4B. Still: June was the biggest revenue month in company history, and the quarter beat the $10.9B internal projection that leaked on May 20. The honest June range is $4.4–5.6B with ~$5.0B a fair central guess. The clearer slowdown came a month later: the confirmed $65B end-of-July run rate sits barely above the ~$63B estimated June exit, putting July growth in the low single digits after a quarter compounding above 20% a month.

Read the fine print

  • Run rates aren't audited revenue, and definitions differ. Bloomberg itself flags that OpenAI and Anthropic "may not measure it the same way" — OpenAI's figure excludes Microsoft licensing and one-time deals; Anthropic's is ~80% API/enterprise. Treat the crossing as approximate.
  • Quarter × 4 ≠ exit run rate. Anthropic's Q2 (>$11.5B) annualizes to ~$46B — but that is the quarter's average pace, which in a fast-growing quarter matches the mid-quarter rate, and mid-quarter was mid-May: exactly when Anthropic disclosed $47B. The exit estimate solves the integral: the curve through $30B (Apr 6) and $47B (mid-May) books $4.45B by May 15, leaving $7.05B for the last 47 days — which forces ~21%/month continued growth and an end-of-June instantaneous rate near $63B ($55–70B across the "more than $11.5B" and path assumptions). The gap between $46B and $63B is the growth itself. The confirmed $65B end-of-July figure validates the estimate — and, read against it, says July grew only ~3% (or ~5–12% if the true June exit sat in the lower half of the band): the sharp deceleration the June data only hinted at arrived in July.
  • Run rate ≠ recognized revenue. Applied to Q1, checkpoint math yields ~$3.9B against Anthropic's actual $4.73B — lumpy enterprise deals land on top. OpenAI similarly booked $5.7B in Q1 2026 (≈$22.8B annualized) while run-rate reports said ~$25B.
  • The mid-2026 OpenAI path (~$25B Feb–Apr) rests on aggregator reporting, not primary articles; the $20B (Dec) and $40B+ (Aug) bookends are solid.
  • Microsoft's "AI business" is a different animal: it bundles Copilot subscriptions with Azure AI infrastructure — including serving OpenAI's models — so its $37B partly double-counts the same economic activity behind OpenAI's line. Comparable as a disclosed number, not as a rival lab.
  • Google and Meta are estimates, not disclosures — public doesn't mean broken out. Alphabet reports at segment level (Services / Cloud); Gemini is not a segment, and Alphabet gives only breadcrumbs: gen-AI product revenue "up ~800% YoY" with no dollar base, 8M paid Gemini Enterprise seats, subscriptions blended into a 350M-sub bundle. The $3–6B stitches those breadcrumbs together and leans on weakly-sourced subscription estimates. Meta's plotted ~$0.1–0.3B reflects that Llama API and Meta AI have no visible live monetization; its leaked $2–3B internal "generative AI" target for 2025 (court filing) has undefined scope.
  • Both giants' real AI revenue is in ads and can't be isolated: Google says AI Overviews/AI Mode monetize at rates similar to Search (and may partly cannibalize it — answers without clicks); Meta's Advantage+ AI ad suite runs at $75B annualized. Neither belongs on a lab-revenue chart, but it is why the gray lines understate what AI earns these two.
  • xAI is messy: $3.2B FY2025 company-wide (includes X platform revenue, per the SpaceX IPO filing) vs ~$500M mid-2026 for Grok standalone. The chart plots Grok standalone.
  • Anthropic's June point and monthly bars are estimates (dashed/hollow on the charts); 2026 targets companies have announced (Mistral €1B, xAI $2B) are excluded — disclosed actuals only.
